Google Maps Data Extraction Guide

How to extract business data from Google Maps: available fields, search planning for full coverage, deduplication, emails, the Places API and compliance.

NSNithin Singh Published 2026-11-148 min readLead Generation
Quick answer

Google Maps data extraction collects public business listing details, such as name, category, address, phone, website, rating and review count, for a category and location. Complete coverage depends on splitting large areas into many smaller searches and deduplicating the merged results. Emails are not usually on Maps and are collected from business websites instead.

What data is available

A typical Google Maps listing can show:

  • Business name and primary category
  • Full address and map coordinates
  • Phone number and website
  • Rating and review count
  • Opening hours
  • The listing's Maps URL, which is useful as a unique identifier

Not every business fills in every field, so expect some blanks. That is the source, not an extraction error.

The coverage problem

A search on Google Maps shows a limited number of results, however many businesses exist. A search for plumbers in California returns only a fraction of the plumbers in California. Full coverage comes from planning:

  1. List every city, or every postcode, in the target region.
  2. For dense cities, split further into neighbourhoods or map grid points.
  3. Run each keyword in each area, plus close synonyms (for example plumber, plumbing service, emergency plumber).
  4. Merge everything and deduplicate.

Deduplication done properly

Overlapping searches return the same business many times. Deduplicate on the Maps listing URL or place ID first, then on normalised phone numbers, then on a normalised name and address combination, to catch the same business listed twice.

Getting email addresses

Email addresses are rarely shown on Maps listings. The standard approach is enrichment: visit each listed website and look for publicly published emails on the homepage, contact page and footer. Expect a portion of businesses to have no public email at all. Read more in How to build a B2B lead generation dataset.

Scraping vs the Places API

Google offers the official Places API, which returns structured place data under Google's terms and pricing. For some projects, especially ongoing integrations inside a product, the API is the right choice. For one-off research lists, browser-based extraction of public listings is commonly used. A good freelancer should tell you which suits your case.

Compliance considerations

Collecting business information that is shown publicly is different from collecting private data. Platform terms still apply, and so do data protection laws (GDPR, UK GDPR, CAN-SPAM and others) when you use the data for outreach. Keep outreach relevant, honour opt-outs and check your obligations in your region. This is general information, not legal advice.

Frequently asked questions

How many results can you get from Google Maps?

As many as exist for your category and area, provided the search is split into enough small areas. A single search is capped; planned searches are not.

Does Google Maps data include emails?

Usually not. Emails are found by visiting each business's own website and collecting publicly published addresses.
