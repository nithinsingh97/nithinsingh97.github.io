What is it?

Google Maps business data extraction collects publicly listed business information, such as name, category, address, phone number, website, rating and review count, for a chosen category and set of locations, and delivers it as a structured spreadsheet for lead generation or market research.

Overview

Google Maps is one of the most complete public sources of local business information. For sales teams, agencies and researchers, a well-built list of businesses by category and city is often the starting point for outreach or market sizing.

The quality of the list depends on how the search is planned. One search for 'dentists in Texas' misses most results. I break the area into cities, postcodes or grid points, run each query, merge the results and remove duplicates, so coverage is as complete as the source allows. Where the official Google Places API fits your use case and budget, I will tell you so.

Business use cases

  • Local business lead lists for agencies, SaaS and B2B sales teams
  • Market sizing: how many businesses of a type exist in a region
  • Competitor mapping and location analysis
  • Enriching an existing list with phone numbers, websites and categories

Example deliverables

  • Spreadsheet with business name, category, address, city, postcode, phone, website, rating and review count (fields depend on what is publicly listed)
  • Google Maps URL for each listing
  • Deduplicated results across overlapping searches
  • Optional enrichment: public emails and social links from business websites

Technologies used

  • Pandas
  • Geographic query planning

Frequently asked questions

Can you extract emails from Google Maps?

Google Maps listings rarely show email addresses. Emails are usually found by visiting each business's own website, which I can do as an enrichment step when the email is publicly published there.

How do you avoid missing businesses?

I split large areas into smaller searches, such as cities, postcodes or map grid points, then merge and deduplicate the results. That gives far better coverage than a single broad search.

Is this compliant?

I collect only publicly displayed business information. Platform terms and local data protection rules such as GDPR still apply to how the data is used, so we should discuss your use case, and you remain responsible for lawful outreach.
